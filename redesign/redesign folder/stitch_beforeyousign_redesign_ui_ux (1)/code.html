Residential Lease Agreement

This Residential Lease Agreement ("Agreement") is made and entered into this 1st day of August, 2024, by and between Texas Property Management LLC ("Landlord") and Jane Doe ("Tenant").

1. PROPERTY

Landlord hereby leases to Tenant, and Tenant hereby leases from Landlord, the premises located at 123 Longhorn Lane, Austin, TX 78701 (the "Premises").

2. TERM

The term of this lease shall be for 12 months, commencing on September 1, 2024, and ending on August 31, 2025.

3. RENT & LATE FEES

Tenant agrees to pay Landlord as base rent the sum of $1,800 per month, due and payable on the 1st day of each month. If rent is not received by the 3rd of the month, a late fee of $100 plus $20 per day thereafter will be assessed.

4. SECURITY DEPOSIT

Upon execution of this Agreement, Tenant shall deposit with Landlord the sum of $2,000 as a security deposit. Landlord may deduct from the security deposit any costs for professional carpet cleaning, repainting, and administrative turnover fees upon move-out, regardless of the condition of the Premises.

5. MAINTENANCE & REPAIRS

Tenant shall keep the Premises in a clean and good condition. Tenant is responsible for the first $150 of any repair requested, including plumbing, HVAC, and electrical issues, regardless of fault.

6. ENTRY BY LANDLORD

Landlord may enter the Premises for inspection, repair, or to show the property to prospective tenants. Landlord will attempt to provide notice but reserves the right to enter without notice during normal business hours.

Deposit Risk visibility

This affects your deposit.

The lease states they can deduct 'administrative turnover fees' and standard painting costs. Under Texas law, deposits generally cannot be used for normal wear and tear.

Maintenance Cost visibility

You might pay for things you didn't break.

Clause 5 makes you responsible for the first $150 of ANY repair. This is unusual and shifts a lot of normal upkeep cost onto you.

gavel Texas Renter Check

error

Late fees might exceed legal limits.

Texas Property Code restricts late fees unless they are a reasonable estimate of damages. $100 + $20/day on an $1800 rent could be challenged if excessive.

info

Landlord Entry Notice.

Texas law doesn't specify an exact notice period, but leases typically grant 24 hours. This lease allows entry "without notice," which is aggressive.
